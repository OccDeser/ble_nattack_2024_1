\section{Related Work} \label{sec:7related-work}

Early studies~\cite{gong1993protecting,kelsey1997secure} have highlighted the profound impact of low-entropy keys on the security of cryptographic protocols. In practical applications, numerous severe security vulnerabilities have been attributed to cryptographic variables with low entropy. Beyond the KNOB and PMKID attacks examined in detail in this paper, vulnerabilities such as the Weak IV attack~\cite{fluhrer2001weaknesses} against the WEP protocol and the Group Guessing Attack~\cite{jangid2023bluepe} against the Bluetooth Passkey Entry Pairing protocol are similarly rooted in low-entropy keys.

Inspired by real-world attacks, several works enhance attackers' capabilities in symbolic models to achieve automated attack finding.
These works focus on attacks due to the weaknesses of specific cryptographic primitives, like hash~\cite{cheval2023hash}, Diffie-Hellman groups~\cite{cremers2019prime}, data signature~\cite{jackson2019sign}, or AEAD schemes~\cite{cremers2023aead}. 
Brute-force attack automatically finding we consider in this work are not covered by these works, and have not been systematically studied in symbolic models. 

Shi et al.~\cite{shi2023blesc} proposed an ad-hoc approach to discover brute-force associated attacks in BLE.
In their analysis, a fixed brute force attack was modeled, where the attacker can compromise a specific key. 
Claverie et al.~\cite{claverie2023tamarin} utilized a similar approach to enable attackers to compromise a certain cryptographic variable in the BLE pairing protocol.
These modeling approaches are tailored to known attacks and can only be used to analyze those specific attacks, making it challenging for them to be effective in discovering potential vulnerabilities.

Siedlecka-Lamch et al.~\cite{siedlecka2016probabilistic} developed a probabilistic tool to model checking of security protocols. 
Given the probability of compromising a key, the tool can automatically generate a probabilistic tree for the secure protocol and output a risk assessment of sensitive information leakage.
This probabilistic approach can only be used to analyze the importance of keys.
Instead, our methodology can be applied to the formal verification of protocols and the automated discovery of attacks. 

In terms of case studies, our approach identified a novel attack on Bluetooth protocols that is not utilized by any known attacks \cite{antonioli2019knob,antonioli2020bias,antonioli2020key,von2021method,antonioli2022blurtooth,antonioli2023bluffs,zhang2020breaking}. 
This attack combines the adversary's man-in-the-middle capabilities with brute force capabilities, which has not been identified in previous work.
