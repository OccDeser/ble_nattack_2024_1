\section{ Case studies } \label{sec:5case-study}

In this section, to demonstrate the effectiveness of our methodology, we will conduct case studies of several real-world protocols that have issues with low-entropy keys. 
In our case studies, we consider an adversary model that builds upon the classical Dolev-Yao model. 
In addition to the standard abilities of a Dolev-Yao attacker, our adversary is equipped with the capability to execute brute-force attacks on all low-entropy keys.
The source code of our tool and models can be found in our repository~\cite{toolrepo}.

\subsection{Case 1: WPA2-PSK} \label{sec:wpa2-psk}

% \textbf{PMKID attack:} 
The security of WPA2-PSK is ensured by the $PMK$. 
The first message of the 4-way-handshake includes $PMKID$ which is calculated from the user chosen $Passphrase$, see~\autoref{eq:pmkid}. 
However, if the user chooses a simple $Passphrase$, the entropy of the PMK will be reduced, allowing an attacker to brute-force $PMK$ from $PMKID$. 
In this case, the attacker can compromise the $PTK$ and $GTK$ through the $PMK$, thereby compromising the security of the entire session. 
This attack is known as the PMKID attack\cite{pmkidattack} and is shown in \autoref{fig:pmkid-attack}. 


% 此外在WPA2-PSK中，PMK(PSK)仅需要用户选择的passphrase便可以计算，因此PMK的熵取决于Passphrase的熵。我们假设存在一个缺乏安全意识的用户，总是选择短且简单的passphrase，这样就导致了PMK的熵较低，我们在模型中添加了这一假设，将PMK标记为低熵的密钥。
\textbf{Security assumption:} 
% in WPA2-PSK, the $PMK$ is calculated from the user-selected $Passphrase$, resulting in the $PMK$'s entropy being contingent on the $Passphrase$'s entropy.
We assume the existence of a security-conscious user who always configures weak $Passphrase$ on the authenticator and the supplicant.

% cremers_wpa2 提出了WPA2的 4-way handshake协议的形式化模型，该模型包括从PMK派生PTK和GTK的过程。由于该模型主要为了验证密钥重装攻击，因此在第一条握手消息中省略了PMKID，我们补全了这一部分：
\textbf{Protocol modeling:}  
\cite{cremers2020wpa2} proposed a formal model of the WPA2, including the process of distributing the $PTK$ and $GTK$ with $PMK$.
As the model primarily focuses on verifying key reinstallation attacks, the $PMKID$ is omitted in the first handshake message.
We added $PMKID$ to the first message of the handshake as follows:
\begin{flalign*}
    \text{\ruleNameText{Rule }}    & \text{\ruleNameText{AuthSndM1}} :                                          & \\
    \TLetS                          & \text{\Tterm{$PMK$ := `L`}}                                                 \\  
                                    & \text{\Tterm{$PMKID$ = HMAC("PMK Name", $PMK$)}}                              \\  
    \TInS \TFacts{                  & \text{\TFactTerm{AuthState}{"INIT", $PMK$}}, \; \text{\TFactTerm{Fr}{$ANonce$}}}  \\
    \TEmpActFact{} \hspace{0.2em} \TFacts{& \text{\TFactTerm{AuthState}{"START", \TPairN{$PMK$, $ANonce$}}},            \\
                                    & \text{\TFactTerm{AuthOut}{\TPairN{$ANonce$, $PMKID$}}}}
\end{flalign*}
Here, we incorporate our security assumption into the model, marking the $PMK$ as a low-entropy key.

\textbf{Property specification:} We will verify the secrecy of $PTK$ to analyze whether attackers can exploit a low-entropy $PMK$ to compromise the security of WPA2-PSK.
We introduce a fact, \TFact{SuppInsPTK}, indicating that the supplicant has installed a $PTK$.
The formal definition of the secrecy of the $PTK$ is as follows:
\begin{flalign*}
    & \hspace{0em} \TNot \, (\TEx \;\, \text{\Tterm{suppID  PTK \#i \#j.}} \;\, &\\
    & \hspace{1em} \text{\TFact{K(PTK)@\#i}} \; \land \; \text{\TFact{SuppInsPTK(suppID, PTK)@\#j}})
\end{flalign*}
This formula indicates that the attacker can't know a $PTK$ while the supplicant has installed it.

\subsection{Case 2: The Paring Protocol of BLE}
In the BLE pairing protocol, there is a KNOB attack\cite{antonioli2019knob}. 
Through this attack, an attacker can downgrade the negotiated key length between the pairing parties, thereby enabling the attacker to compromise the long-term key through brute force.
As illustrated in \autoref{fig:ble-knob}, the attacker manipulates the $LTK$ length field within the pairing features exchanged between the Initiator and Responder, leading to the negotiation of a shorter $LTK$.
This alteration substantially reduces the complexity of brute-forcing $LTK$.

\textbf{Security assumption:} 
The Bluetooth core specification\cite{bluetooth6} states that Bluetooth Low Energy devices can support key lengths of 7 to 16 bytes. For convenience, we assume that the devices involved in pairing support key lengths of 8 to 16 bytes. In this scenario, without the involvement of an attacker, these devices will negotiate a full-length LTK.
Additionally, we assume that the attacker can brute-force an 8-byte key, meaning the attacker can quickly traverse the key space of 8 bytes or fewer.
\BlueQuote{[Vol 3, Part H, 2.3.4]}{ 
The maximum and minimum encryption key length parameters shall be between 7 octets (56 bits) and 16 octets (128 bits), in 1 octet (8~bit) steps.}

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{img/ble-knob} 
    \caption{BLE KNOB}
    \label{fig:ble-knob}
\end{figure}

% \cite{shi2023formal} 开发了BLE-SC配对协议的的全面的形式化模型，涵盖了规范中所有的关联模型。根据蓝牙标准，计算LTK的最后一步是根据协商的密钥长度对LTK进行截取，例如，协商的密钥长度为7字节，那么LTK只会保留7字节的有效数据。考虑到攻击者的计算能力，长度较短的LTK是可以被攻击者获取的，而完整长度的LTK无法被攻击者直接破解。现有的工作直接定义了如下的rule，给攻击者得到低熵密钥的能力：
\textbf{Protocol modeling:}
\cite{shi2023blesc} has developed a comprehensive formal model for the BLE-SC pairing protocol, which includes all related models specified in the standards.
According to the Bluetooth Specification, the final process in $LTK$ derivation involves truncating the $LTK$ to the negotiated key length.
For instance, if the negotiated key length is 7 bytes, only 7 bytes of the $LTK$ will remain as valid data.
Given the computational capabilities of an adversary, shorter $LTK$s are susceptible to being compromised, whereas a full-length $LTK$ remains secure against brute-force attacks.
Existing research defines a rule that provides the attacker with the ability to access low-entropy keys:
\begin{flalign*}
    \text{\ruleNameText{Rule R}}    & \text{\ruleNameText{evealLTK}} :                                   & \\
                            \TLetS  & \text{\Tterm{$LTK$ = resize(\_, "Low Entropy")}}       \\
     \TInS \TFacts{                 & \text{\TFactTerm{In}{senc(m, $LTK$)}}} \TEmpActFact{} \TFacts{ \text{\TFactTerm{Out}{$LTK$}}}             
\end{flalign*}
% 其中，resize函数代表了LTK的截断操作，第一个参数是待截断的密钥，第二个参数是截断后的密钥长度。虽然这条规则虽然赋予了攻击者获取低熵的LTK的能力，但是这条规则并没有还原真实的攻击行为。事实上，配对的蓝牙设备在使用LTK保护通信时，会首先使用LTK派生出SK，然后使用SK加密发往公开信道的数据。delov-yao模型中的攻击者无法获取到使用LTK加密的密文，也就无法使用这条规则来妥协低熵的LTK。这样的模型可以发现KNOB攻击，是因为这个模型简化了SK的参与，直接使用了LTK来保护通信数据。我们希望更加还原协议的行为，因此补充了LTK参与派生SK，以及SK用于保护通信。
The \funcText{resize} function is designed to model the truncation operation of $LTK$, with the first parameter being the key to be truncated and the second parameter specifying the desired length of the truncated key.
While this rule provides the attacker with the theoretical ability to obtain a low-entropy $LTK$, it does not accurately replicate the actual attack scenario.
In fact, paired Bluetooth devices utilize the $LTK$ to secure communications by first deriving a Session Key (SK) from the $LTK$, and subsequently employing the SK to encrypt data transmitted over the public channel.
In the Dolev-Yao model, the attacker is unable to intercept the ciphertext encrypted with the $LTK$, thus making it impossible to exploit this rule to compromise a low-entropy $LTK$.
The model's ability to discover the KNOB attack arises from its simplification of SK involvement, where the $LTK$ is directly used to secure communication data.
In our efforts to more faithfully model the protocol's execution, we have incorporated the derivation of the SK from the $LTK$ and the use of the SK in securing communications.

Furthermore, we modeled the \funcText{resize} function with a finer granularity.
From the attacker's perspective, we define two key lengths, $N_1$ and $N_2$, where $N_1$ denotes the key lengths susceptible to attack, and $N_2$ denotes those remaining secure.
To provide a detailed model of the $LTK$ truncation process, we introduce two functions, \funcText{left} and \funcText{right}, which respectively denote the left and right halves of the key.
The \funcText{resize} function satisfies the following equations:

\begin{flalign*}
    \text{\Tterm{resize(k, $N_1$)}} & \text{ = \Tterm{left(k)}}    & \\
    \text{\Tterm{resize(k, $N_2$)}} & \text{ = \TPairN{left(k), right(k)}}    
\end{flalign*}

% LTK派生的过程建模如下：
Taking into account the computational capabilities of an adversary, both \Tterm{left(LTK')} and \Tterm{right(LTK')} are vulnerable from the attacker's perspective
Therefore, in our model, both of these $LTK$ parts are marked as low-entropy keys.
The $LTK$ truncation process is modeled as follows:
\begin{flalign*}
    \text{\ruleNameText{Rule R}}    & \text{\ruleNameText{esizeLTK}} :                                   & \\
        \TLetS                  & \text{\Tterm{LHalf, RHalf := `L`}}                     \\
                                & \text{\Tterm{$LTK$ = resize(LTK', KS)}}             \\
                                & \text{\Tterm{LHalf = left(LTK')}}                  \\
                                & \text{\Tterm{RHalf = right(LTK')}}                 \\
        \TInS \TFacts{          & \text{\TFactTerm{InitState}{"CALCING", \TPairN{LTK', KS}}}}   \\
        \TEmpActFact{ } \hspace{0.5em} \TFacts{ & \text{\TFactTerm{InitState}{"CALCED", $LTK$}}}                  
\end{flalign*}
In this rule, \Tterm{KS} denotes the negotiated key length, while \Tterm{LHalf} and \Tterm{RHalf} represent the left and right halves of \Tterm{LTK'} respectively.
They are labeled as low-entropy keys.

% 我们聚焦于分析攻击者能否利用计算能力破解密钥，因此我们着重研究协议中密钥的保密性。我们定义了两个事实，\TFact{LTKNegotiated} 与 \TFact{SKDistributed}，分别代表了LTK成功被协商出来，SK被成功分发。LTK与SK的保密性分别定义如下：
\textbf{Property specification:}
Our focus is on analyzing whether an attacker can leverage computational capabilities to compromise keys, thus we emphasize the study of key confidentiality within the protocol.
We define two facts, \TFact{LtkNegotiated} and \TFact{SkDistributed}, representing the successful negotiation of $LTK$ and the successful distribution of the SK, respectively.
The secrecy of $LTK$ for the Initiator and Responder is defined as follows:
% ![注意，这里的式子有点复杂，需要分解
\begin{flalign*}
    & \hspace{0em} \TAll \;\, \text{\Tterm{Addr LTK \#i.}} \;\, &\\
    & \hspace{1em} \text{\TFact{LtkNegotiated(Role, Addr, LTK)@\#i}} \\
    & \hspace{0em} \implies \TNot (\TEx \;\, \text{\TLemma{\#j.}} \;\, \text{\TFact{K(LTK)@\#j}} ) 
\end{flalign*}
where \TFact{Role} could be ``Initiator'' or ``Responder''.
This formula indicates that it is impossible for the attacker to know the $LTK$ once the initiator or responder has successfully negotiated it.
Similarly, the Secrecy of $SK$ means the attacker cannot know the $SK$ while the initiator or responder has successfully negotiated it, shown as follows:
\begin{flalign*}
    & \hspace{0em} \TAll \;\, \text{\Tterm{Addr SK \#i.}} \;\, &\\
    & \hspace{1em} \text{\TFact{SkDistributed(Role, Addr, SK)@\#i}} \\
    & \hspace{0em} \implies \TNot (\TEx \;\, \text{\TLemma{\#j.}} \;\, \text{\TFact{K(SK)@\#j}} ) 
\end{flalign*}

\subsection{Case 3: The Session Establishment of BC} \label{sec:bc-devices}

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.962747\columnwidth]{img/bc-knob} 
    \caption{BC KNOB}
    \label{fig:bc-knob}
\end{figure}

% BC设备同样会受到KNOB攻击，因为在建立加密会话时，BC会协商会话密钥的长度。 在\autoref{fig:bc-knob}中展示了KNOB攻击的过程，攻击者通过篡改中心设备与边缘设备协商的会话密钥长度，使得会话密钥长度较短，从而降低了攻击者通过暴力破解会话密钥的难度。在蓝牙规范中，BC设备支持的会话密钥长度可以是1-16字节，在一些情况下，攻击者事实KNOB攻击，可以最短将会话密钥长度降级到1字节。
\textbf{KNOB attack:}  
BC devices are  also vulnerable to KNOB attacks due to their negotiation of session key lengths during the establishment of sessions.
The mechanism of the KNOB attack is depicted in \autoref{fig:bc-knob}.
An adversary can alter the negotiated session key length between the central and peripheral devices, which results in a shortened session key.
This significantly reduces the difficulty of brute-forcing the session key for the attacker.
As per the Bluetooth specification, BC devices support session key lengths in the range of 1 to 16 bytes.
Under certain circumstances, an attacker can effectively implement a KNOB attack, reducing the session key length to 1 byte.

% 在蓝牙specification中，BC设备支持的会话密钥长度从1字节到16字节。在BLE的case中我们提到的两个攻击者SA与QA都可能对BC设备的会话密钥产生威胁，因此我们将在BC的case中考虑这两种攻击者。
\textbf{Security assumption:} 
% 在蓝牙Core Specification v5.1以及之前的版本中，BC设备支持的最短密钥长度可以是1字节。在蓝牙v5.2及之后的规范中建议BC设备支持的最短密钥长度至少为7字节。以下是在最新的蓝牙v6.0标准文件中相关内容的表述：
In the Bluetooth Core Specification version 5.1\cite{bluetooth51} and preceding versions, the minimum key size allowed by BC devices is 1 byte.
However, starting from Bluetooth version 5.2\cite{bluetooth52}, it is advised that BC devices support the minimum key size should not be less than 7 bytes.
The subsequent text is extracted from the most recent Bluetooth Core Specification version 6.0\cite{bluetooth6}, which elaborates on this recommendation.
\BlueQuote{[Vol 2, Part C, 4.2.5.2]}{ 
L\_max\_c and L\_max\_p \emph{shall} be set to at least 7 octets.
L\_min\_c and L\_min\_p \emph{should} be set to at least 7 octets.
}
% 在蓝牙specification中，\textbf{shall} 代表了强制要求，\textbf{should} 代表了建议。因此，支持最大的密钥长度低于7字节的BC设备是不符合规范的。然而，我们在最新的规范中找到了有关BC设备允许的最大密钥长度的另一处表述，如下所示：
In the Bluetooth specification, the term \emph{shall} denotes a mandatory requirement, whereas \emph{should} indicates a recommended practice.
So BC devices that support a maximum key size of less than 7 bytes are deemed non-compliant with the specification.
Nonetheless, in the same specification, we have identified an alternative statement about the permissible maximum key length for BC devices, which is detailed as follows:
\BlueQuote{[Vol 2, Part H, 4.1]}{ 
Each device implementing the Baseband specification shall have a parameter defining the maximal allowed key length, L\_max, $1 \leq \text{L\_max} \leq 16$ (number of octets in the key).}
% 这里表述BC设备支持的最大密钥长度可以是1字节到16字节，与之前的表述明显存在矛盾。
It is indicated that the maximum key length accepted by BC devices ranges from 1 byte to 16 bytes, which contradicts the previous statement.

% 鉴于the specification 对最短的密钥长度并没有严格的要求，而且表述前后混乱，我们假定BC设备支持的密钥长度范围存在三种类型：
Since there is no strict requirement regarding the minimum key length in the specification, and the statements are inconsistent, we assume that there are three types of key length ranges accepted by BC devices:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Secure Keysize:} The BC device only accepts keys of 16 bytes and rejects any keys shorter than 16 bytes.
    \item \textbf{Enhancing Keysize:} The BC device accepts keys ranging from 7 to 16 bytes, but prefers 16-byte keys.
    \item \textbf{Legacy Keysize:} The BC device accepts keys ranging from 1 to 16 bytes, but prefers 16-byte keys.
\end{itemize}
% 此外，按照是否支持SC特性，我们将设备分为SC-Only、SC-Legacy、Legacy-Only，分别代表只支持Secure Connections的设备，同时支持Legacy和Secure Connections的设备，以及只支持Legacy功能的设备。SC特性与密钥支持的特性进行组合，一共有六种设备类型。由于BC的会话加密密钥最低可能低至1字节，在QA模型下并不一定安全，因此我们将同时考虑攻击者二分与四分的攻击能力。
Furthermore, devices are classified according to their support for the SC feature into three categories: SC-Only, SC-Legacy, and Legacy-Only.
These categories correspond to devices that only support Secure Connections, those that support both Legacy and Secure Connections, and those limited to Legacy connections, respectively.
\cite{padgette2017guide} recommends that devices adhere to the following guidelines: only use secure connections and only use the maximum key length.
By integrating the SC feature with key support characteristics, six distinct device types emerge.

% 考虑到最小的密钥长度可以低至1字节，我们假设存在两类攻击者：
% \begin{itemize}
%     \item \textbf{SA:} 攻击者可以破解一半长度的密钥。
%     \item \textbf{QA:} 攻击者可以破解四分之一长度的密钥。
% \end{itemize}
% 这两类攻击者分别代表了计算能力较强的攻击者（可以暴力破解8字节的密钥）与计算能力较弱的攻击者（可以暴力破解4字节的密钥）。
% 我们将在模型中分别考虑SA与QA两种攻击者，以验证BC设备在不同的安全假设下的安全性。
Considering that the minimum key length can be as low as 1 byte, we assume the existence of two types of attackers:
% !
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Semi-Attacker(SA):} An attacker capable of cracking a key that is half the maximum length.
    \item \textbf{Quart-Attacker(QA):} An attacker capable of cracking a key that is a quarter of the maximum length.
\end{itemize}
These two types of attackers represent different levels of computational power: a stronger attacker who can brute-force an 8-byte key and a weaker attacker who can brute-force a 4-byte key.
So the attacker model we consider in BLE is the SA attacker.
We will consider both SA and QA attackers in our model to evaluate the security of BC devices under different security assumptions.

% 我们建模了BC-SC与BC-Legacy进行了全面的形式化建模，以支持我们安全假设中不同的设备类型的验证。我们通过如下的equations来建模协议中设备交换双方的认证需求，来选择会话建立方法的过程：
\textbf{Protocol modeling:}
We have developed a comprehensive formal model for BC's SC and Legacy session establishment protocols to facilitate the verification of various device types under our specified security assumptions.

\begin{flalign*}
    \text{\ruleNameText{Rule R}}    & \text{\ruleNameText{esizeSKQA}}:                                      & \\
    \TLetS                          & \text{\Tterm{l(l(SK')),r(l(SK')) := `L`}} \\
                                    & \text{\Tterm{l(r(SK')),r(r(SK')) := `L`}} \\

Within the attacker model for SA, we consider keys shorter than half the length to be vulnerable by attackers, with corresponding model rules consistent with those in BLE. 
In contrast, under the QA model, only keys shorter than a quarter of the length are considered vulnerable. 
In our rules, we labeled low-entropy keys like the rule above.

% 在BC模型中我们同样考虑了会话密钥的保密性，我们定义了 \TFact{SKDistributed} 事实，分别代表了会话密钥成功被分发。我们定义了如下的规则来验证会话密钥的保密性：
\textbf{Property specification:}
In the BC model, the secrecy of the session key is also a critical consideration.
We define the \TFact{SKDistributed} fact to represent the successful distribution of the session key.
The property is specified as follows:
