The Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) Key Negotiation Downgrade Attack, also known as the BC (Bluetooth Classic) BLE Key Negotiation Downgrade Attack, exposes another facet of how modern attackers can manipulate cryptographic protocols.
This attack exploits a vulnerability in the BLE pairing process where an attacker can force the devices to negotiate a lower level of security, specifically by downgrading the key strength used for encryption.

By intercepting and modifying the key negotiation process, attackers can downgrade the encryption from a stronger key to a weaker one, which is easier to brute-force.
Once the weaker key is in place, the attacker can use brute-force methods to break the encryption, gaining access to the communication channel.
This attack is particularly concerning for IoT devices, which often use BLE for communication and may not have the computational power to implement stronger cryptographic measures.

The BLE Key Negotiation Downgrade Attack highlights the broader issue of how at`tackers can exploit key management flaws to weaken the security of cryptographic protocols, making them vulnerable to brute-force attacks.
It also underscores the importance of ensuring that protocols are resilient not just against brute-force attacks on high-entropy keys, but also against downgrade attacks that reduce key entropy.

\subsection{Case 1: The 4-way Handshake of WPA2-PSK} \label{sec:wpa2-psk}


\subsection{WPA2 PMKID Attack} 
The WPA2 PMKID Attack, discovered in 2018, is a prime example of how attackers can exploit protocol vulnerabilities to facilitate brute-force attacks.
The attack targets the WPA2 handshake process, specifically the PMKID (Pairwise Master Key Identifier) that is transmitted during the initial connection between a client and an access point.
Traditionally, capturing a full four-way handshake was required to launch a brute-force attack on the WPA2 Pre-Shared Key (PSK).
However, the PMKID Attack simplifies this process by allowing attackers to capture the PMKID and then brute-force the PSK offline without needing the full handshake.

This attack is particularly dangerous because it exploits a design flaw in the WPA2 protocol, not just an implementation error.
The availability of tools like Hashcat, which can efficiently perform such brute-force attacks using GPUs, has further lowered the barrier for attackers to successfully compromise Wi-Fi networks.
The PMKID Attack demonstrates how even protocols considered secure can be vulnerable when attackers leverage their brute-force capabilities.
This attack is particularly dangerous because it exploits a design flaw in the WPA2 protocol, not just an implementation error.
The availability of tools like Hashcat, which can efficiently perform such brute-force attacks using GPUs, has further lowered the barrier for attackers to successfully compromise Wi-Fi networks.
The PMKID Attack demonstrates how even protocols considered secure can be vulnerable when attackers leverage their brute-force capabilities.

This attack is known as the ~PMKID attack {cite}.

\subsection{Case 2: The Paring Protocol of BLE}
% Key Negotiation Of Bluetooth(KNOB)攻击在针对低功耗蓝牙时，攻击者可以通过降低LTK的长度来降低密钥被破解的难度，进而破坏通信的安全性。\autoref{fig:ble-knob}展示了KNOB攻击的过程，攻击者通过篡改双方配对feature中的LTK长度字段，使得Initiator和Responder协商出的LTK长度较短，从而降低了攻击者通过暴力破解LTK的难度。
\textbf{KNOB attack:}  
The Key Negotiation of Bluetooth (KNOB) attack poses a significant threat to low-power Bluetooth communications by allowing an attacker to decrease the difficulty of compromising the Long-Term Key (LTK) through the intentional reduction of its length.
This reduction undermines the security of the communication channel.
As illustrated in \autoref{fig:ble-knob}, the attacker manipulates the LTK length field within the pairing features exchanged between the Initiator and Responder, leading to the negotiation of a shorter LTK.
This alteration substantially reduces the complexity of a brute-force attack on the LTK.

\textbf{Security assumption:} 
In the proposed model, it is assumed that the participating BLE devices possess an IO capability of KeyboardDisplay, with a maximum expected key length of 16 bytes as stipulated by the protocol.
In the absence of an adversary, these devices will employ the Numeric Comparison pairing method, achieving a full-length LTK.
To comprehensively analyze the influence of an attacker's computational capabilities on the security of the protocol, we will incrementally loosen the constraint on the minimum key length for BLE devices, while enhancing the attacker's computational capabilities.

We consider two categories of devices:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Extremely Secure Device(ES-D)}: These devices place a high emphasis on security and only accept full-length LTKs.
\item \textbf{Normally Secure Device(NS-D)}: These devices conform to the recommended guidelines, accepting LTKs ranging from 7 to 16 bytes, although they prefer full-length LTKs.
\end{itemize}

With respect to the attacker's computational capabilities, we assume that there are two types of attackers:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Semi-cracking Attacker(SA)}: This attacker can traverse the key space of a key with a length of half the maximum LTK length in a short period, i.e., the attacker has the ability to brute force key of 8 bytes or less.
    \item \textbf{Quart-cracking Attacker(QA)}: Similar to the SA, this attacker can crack keys of 4 bytes or less.
\end{itemize}
The minimum length of LTK is 7 bytes makes it impossible for QA to compromise any LTKs.
So we only take SA into consideration in the case of BLE.

\textbf{Protocol modeling:}
\cite{shi2023blesc} has developed a comprehensive formal model for the BLE-SC pairing protocol, which includes all related models specified in the standards.
According to the Bluetooth Specification, the final process in LTK derivation involves truncating the LTK to the negotiated key length.
For instance, if the negotiated key length is 7 bytes, only 7 bytes of the LTK will remain as valid data.
Given the computational capabilities of an adversary, shorter LTKs are susceptible to being compromised, whereas a full-length LTK remains secure against brute-force attacks.
Existing research defines a rule that provides the attacker with the ability to access low-entropy keys:

\end{flalign*}

The \funcText{resize} function is designed to model the truncation operation of LTK, with the first parameter being the key to be truncated and the second parameter specifying the desired length of the truncated key.
While this rule provides the attacker with the theoretical ability to obtain a low-entropy LTK, it does not accurately replicate the actual attack scenario.
In fact, paired Bluetooth devices utilize the LTK to secure communications by first deriving a Session Key (SK) from the LTK, and subsequently employing the SK to encrypt data transmitted over the public channel.
In the Dolev-Yao model, the attacker is unable to intercept the ciphertext encrypted with the LTK, thus making it impossible to exploit this rule to compromise a low-entropy LTK.
The model's ability to discover the KNOB attack arises from its simplification of SK involvement, where the LTK is directly used to secure communication data.
In our efforts to more faithfully model the protocol's execution, we have incorporated the derivation of the SK from the LTK and the use of the SK in securing communications.

\textbf{KNOB attack:}  
BC devices are  also vulnerable to KNOB attacks due to their negotiation of session key lengths during the establishment of sessions.
The mechanism of the KNOB attack is depicted in \autoref{fig:bc-knob}.
An adversary can alter the negotiated session key length between the central and peripheral devices, which results in a shortened session key.
This significantly reduces the difficulty of brute-forcing the session key for the attacker.
As per the Bluetooth specification, BC devices support session key lengths in the range of 1 to 16 bytes.
Under certain circumstances, an attacker can effectively implement a KNOB attack, reducing the session key length to 1 byte.

% 在蓝牙specification中，BC设备支持的会话密钥长度从1字节到16字节。在BLE的case中我们提到的两个攻击者SA与QA都可能对BC设备的会话密钥产生威胁，因此我们将在BC的case中考虑这两种攻击者。
\textbf{Security assumption:} 
% 在蓝牙Core Specification v5.1以及之前的版本中，BC设备支持的最短密钥长度可以是1字节。在蓝牙v5.2及之后的规范中建议BC设备支持的最短密钥长度至少为7字节。以下是在最新的蓝牙v6.0标准文件中相关内容的表述：
In the Bluetooth Core Specification version 5.1 and preceding versions, the minimum key size can be allowed by BC devices is 1 byte.
However, starting from Bluetooth version 5.2, it is advised that BC devices support the minimum key size should not be less than 7 bytes.
The subsequent text is extracted from the most recent Bluetooth Core Specification version 6.0, which elaborates on this recommendation.
\BlueQuote{[Vol 2, Part C, 4.2.5.2]}{ 
    L\_max\_c and L\_max\_p \textbf{shall} be set to at least 7 octets.
L\_min\_c and L\_min\_p \textbf{should} be set to at least 7 octets.

}
% 在蓝牙specification中，\textbf{shall} 代表了强制要求，\textbf{should} 代表了建议。因此，支持最大的密钥长度低于7字节的BC设备是不符合规范的。然而，我们在最新的规范中找到了有关BC设备允许的最大密钥长度的另一处表述，如下所示：
In the Bluetooth specification, the term \textbf{shall} denotes a mandatory requirement, whereas \textbf{should} indicates a recommended practice.
So BC devices that support a maximum key size of less than 7 bytes are deemed non-compliant with the specification.
Nonetheless, in the same specification, we have identified an alternative statement pertaining to the permissible maximum key length for BC devices, which is detailed as follows:
\BlueQuote{[Vol 2, Part H, 4.1]}{ \\
Each device implementing the Baseband specification shall have a parameter defining the maximal allowed key length, L\_max, $1 \leq \text{L\_max} \leq 16$ (number of octets in the key).}
% 这里表述BC设备支持的最大密钥长度可以是1字节到16字节，与之前的表述明显存在矛盾。
It is indicated that the maximum key length accepted by BC devices ranges from 1 byte to 16 bytes, which clearly contradicts the previous statement.

% 鉴于the specification 对最短的密钥长度并没有严格的要求，而且表述前后混乱，我们假定BC设备支持的密钥长度范围存在三种类型：
Since there is no strict requirement regarding the minimum key length in the specification, and the statements are inconsistent, we assume that there are three types of key length ranges accepted by BC devices:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Secure Keysize:} The BC device only accepts keys of 16 bytes and rejects any keys shorter than 16 bytes.
    \item \textbf{Enhancing Keysize:} The BC device accepts keys ranging from 7 to 16 bytes, but prefers 16-byte keys.
    \item \textbf{Legacy Keysize:} The BC device accepts keys ranging from 1 to 16 bytes, but prefers 16-byte keys.
\end{itemize}
% 此外，按照是否支持SC特性，我们将设备分为SC-Only、SC-Legacy、Legacy-Only，分别代表只支持Secure Connections的设备，同时支持Legacy和Secure Connections的设备，以及只支持Legacy功能的设备。SC特性与密钥支持的特性进行组合，一共有六种设备类型。由于BC的会话加密密钥最低可能低至1字节，在QA模型下并不一定安全，因此我们将同时考虑攻击者二分与四分的攻击能力。
Furthermore, devices are classified according to their support for the SC feature into three categories: SC-Only, SC-Legacy, and Legacy-Only.
These categories correspond to devices that only support Secure Connections, those that support both Legacy and Secure Connections, and those limited to Legacy connections, respectively.
By integrating the SC feature with key support characteristics, six distinct device types emerge.
Considering that the session encryption key for BC can be as short as 1 byte, there are potential security concerns within the QA model.
Therefore, both SA and QA model of the adversary will be taken into account.

\textbf{Protocol modeling:}
We have developed a comprehensive formal model for BC's SC and Legacy session establishment protocols to facilitate the verification of various device types under our specified security assumptions.The procedure for selecting a session establishment method according to the two devices' SC feature are represented by the following equations:
\begin{flalign*}
    \text{\Tterm{Select(FALSE, \_)}} &= \text{\Tterm{LegacyMethod}} & \\
    \text{\Tterm{Select(\_, FALSE)}} &= \text{\Tterm{LegacyMethod}} \\
    \text{\Tterm{Select(TRUE, TRUE)}} &= \text{\Tterm{SecureMethod}} 
\end{flalign*}

In this context, \Tterm{TRUE}, \Tterm{FALSE}, \Tterm{SecureMethod}, and \Tterm{LegacyMethod} are 0-arity functions.
The functions \Tterm{TRUE} and \Tterm{FALSE} denote whether a device supports SC.
Meanwhile, \Tterm{ScMethod} and \Tterm{LyMethod} specify the methods selected by the device for session establishment, respectively.

\textbf{Property specification:}
In the BC model, the secrecy of the session key is also a critical consideration.
We define the \TFact{SKDistributed} fact to represent the successful distribution of the session key.
The property is specified as follows:
\begin{flalign*}

    & \hspace{0em} \implies \neg (\text{\TLemma{Ex \#j.}} \;\, \text{\TFact{K(SK)@j}} ) \\
\end{flalign*}
where \TFact{Role} could be either "Central" or "Peripheral".
This formula indicates that it is impossible for the attacker to know the session key while the central or peripheral device has successfully distributed it.
