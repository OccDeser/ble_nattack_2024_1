\subsection{Entropy and brute force attacks}

% 熵是信息论中的一个重要概念，由香农在1948年提出，用于衡量系统的随机性和不确定性。在密码学中，密钥的熵是衡量密钥强度的重要指标，一个在密钥空间均匀分布的密钥的熵通常可以用以下的公式表示：
% H = log_2(N)
% 其中N是密钥空间的大小。该公式说明了，均匀分布的密钥的熵就是该密钥的比特数。那么对于想要通过穷尽搜索的方式找到密钥的攻击者来说，他需要尝试的密钥的数量就是2^H，其中H是密钥的熵。因此，密钥的熵越高，攻击者找到密钥的难度就越大。
Entropy was introduced by Shannon in 1948 \cite{shannon1948communicationtheory}, to quantify the randomness and uncertainty within a system.
In the domain of cryptography, the entropy of a key serves as a crucial metric for assessing its strength.
Formally, consider a uniformly distributed key~$K$ within a key space of size~$N$.
The entropy of such a key can be represented by \autoref{eq:entropy_definition}.
\begin{equation} 
    H(K) = \log_2(N) \label{eq:entropy_definition}
\end{equation}
where~$H(K)$ denotes the entropy of the key~$K$.
This equation illustrates that the entropy of a uniformly distributed key is equivalent to its bit length.
For an adversary employing exhaustive search techniques to locate the key, the number of keys that must be tested is~$2^{H(K)}$.
Therefore, an increase in the key's entropy directly correlates with a higher level of difficulty for an attacker attempting to restore the key.

% 这种遍历密钥空间的方法，不断地尝试密钥，直到找到正确的密钥，就是暴力破解攻击。通过是否需要与目标系统进行实时交互可以分为在线攻击与离线攻击。在线的攻击，攻击者需要不断地将尝试的密钥发送给目标系统以判断密钥是否正确。离线攻击，通常是通过获取目标系统的密文，攻击者可以在不与目标系统交互的情况下，尝试所有的密钥通过解密密文的方式来鉴别密钥。通常来说在线的暴力破解攻击主要受限于网络速度和目标系统的响应时间，而离线的攻击主要受限于攻击者的计算速度。离线暴力破解攻击的运行效率完全掌握在攻击者手中，密钥因此需要具有足够的熵来抵御这种攻击。因此本文主要研究的就是离线的暴力破解攻击，后文提到的暴力破解攻击如果没有特别制定，均指离线的暴力破解攻击。
The method of exhaustively searching the key space by continually attempting different keys until the correct one is identified as a brute-force attack.
Brute-force attacks can be classified into two categories, online and offline attacks, based on the necessity for real-time interaction with the target system.
In an online attack, the adversary must persistently transmit the attempted keys to the target system to verify their correctness.
% Conversely, in an offline attack, the adversary acquires the ciphertext or other calculation result from the target system and can attempt all possible keys by decrypting the ciphertext, thereby obviating the need for interaction with the target system.
In an offline one, the adversary obtains the ciphertext and can try all possible keys to decrypt it, without needing to interact with the target system.

Typically, the efficiency of online brute-force attacks is constrained by network latency and the response time of the target system and offline attacks are predominantly limited by the attacker's computational resources.
The operational efficiency of offline brute-force attacks is entirely governed by the attacker's capabilities; therefore, the cryptographic key must possess adequate entropy to resist such attacks.
In this paper, we primarily focuses on the offline brute-force attacks.
Throughout the subsequent sections, any reference to brute-force attacks, unless explicitly stated otherwise, pertains to offline brute-force attacks.

\subsection{Protocols with low-entropy keys} 
Low-entropy keys applied in in cryptographic protocols are not rare in practice. 
Just as we mentioned before, the compatibility for old devices, the power consumption for low-power devices, and the user experience prevent protocols from guaranteeing all keys of them possess high entropy.
The use of low-entropy keys is a trade-off between security and practicality. 
A lot of wide used protocols have low-entropy keys in them, such as WPA2-PSK, Bluetooth Low Energy and Bluetooth Classic.

\subsubsection{WPA2-PSK}
% WPA与WPA2源于IEEE 802.11i标准，其中WPA作为过渡性解决方案，旨在修复WEP（Wired Equivalent Privacy）的安全漏洞，而WPA2则完整实现了IEEE 802.11i标准，提供更为完善的安全机制。WPA2-PSK（预共享密钥）是WPA2的一种简化模式，利用预共享密钥代替复杂的企业级认证过程，适用于不需要RADIUS服务器的小型网络。WPA2-PSK广泛应用于家庭、咖啡馆和中小型办公场所，因其配置简便且能够提供较高的安全性。
Wi-Fi Protected Access (WPA) and WPA2 are both derived from the IEEE 802.11i standard \cite{80211i}.
WPA2-PSK (Pre-Shared Key) is a simplified version of WPA2, using a pre-shared key instead of a complex enterprise-level authentication process, making it suitable for small networks without a RADIUS server.
WPA2-PSK is widely deployed in home environments, cafés, and small to medium-sized offices due to its ease of configuration and strong security.

% WPA2的4-way Handshake是一种用于在无线网络中安全地生成和交换加密密钥的协议过程，确保客户端与接入点之间的通信安全。与企业级WPA2认证机制依赖于802.1X和RADIUS服务器进行动态密钥分配的方式有所不同，在WPA2-PSK（Pre-Shared Key）模式下，4-way Handshake利用预共享密钥进行身份验证，
The 4-way handshake of is protocol designed to securely generate and exchange encryption keys within wireless networks, thereby ensuring secure communication between clients and access points.
In contrast to WPA2-Enterprise, which utilizes 802.1X and RADIUS servers for dynamic key distribution, the WPA2-PSK (Pre-Shared Key) employs a pre-shared key for the purpose of authentication.

\begin{figure}[hbtp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.870780\columnwidth]{img/4-way-handshake} 
    \caption{4-way-handshake}
    \label{fig:4-way-handshake}
\end{figure}

% \autoref{fig:4-way-handshake} 展示了用户设备（supplicant）与access point（authenticator）之间通过4-way handshake从PMK协商出PTK与GTK的全部过程。
% WPA2-PSK 使用PBKDF2密钥派生函数，从Passphrase和Service Set Identification (SSID)生成PSK，PBKDF2的定义如下：
% \[
%    PSK = PBKDF2(Passphrase, SSID, 4096, 256)
% \]
% Passphrase 是由用户定义的秘密短语，由8到63个字符组成，SSID是网络的名称，通常是公开的，因此PSK的安全性将取决于Passphrase的强度。在WPA2-PSK中，Pairwise Master Key (PMK)与PSK是一致的。基于PMK，authenticator与supplicant直接执行4-way握手。首先，authenticator发送一个随机数ANonce给supplicant，supplicant收到后，生成自己的随机数SNonce，并计算出PTK。PTK的计算过程如下：
% \[
%     PTK = PRF(PMK, "Pairwise key expansion", ANonce || SNonce || MAC_authenticator || MAC_supplicant)
% \]
% 其中，PRF是伪随机函数，MAC_authenticator和MAC_supplicant分别是authenticator和supplicant的MAC地址，$\{\}_{min\_max}$ 代表了变量以由小到大的顺序拼接 。PTK被用于确保通信的隐私性和完整性。 接下来，authenticator发送GTK给supplicant，用于组播通信的加密密钥。最后，supplicant发送EAPOL消息4给authenticator，用于验证握手的完整性。当authenticator收到EAPOL消息4后，握手完成，supplicant与authenticator之间的通信将使用PTK和GTK进行加密。值得注意的是，根据802.1x 标准，4-way handshake由四条EAPOLkey类型的消息组成，其中，第一条EAPOLkey消息会包含一个额外的字段PMKID，它的计算方式如下：
% \[
%     PMKID = HMAC-SHA1-128(PSK, "PMK Name" || MAC_authenticator || MAC_supplicant)
% \]
% PMKID允许设备在重新连接时迅速完成身份验证。然而，由于PMK（PSK）的强度直接受用户选择的Passphrase决定，如果用户选择了弱Passphrase，攻击者可以通过暴力破解PMKID来获取PSK，从而破坏通信的安全性，这种攻击方式被称为PMKID攻击{cite}。
\autoref{fig:4-way-handshake} illustrates the complete process of negotiating the Pairwise Transient Key~$PTK$ and Group Temporal Key~$GTK$ from the Pairwise Master Key~$PMK$ between the user device (supplicant) and access point (authenticator) via the 4-way handshake.

WPA2-PSK uses the key derivation function PBKDF2 to generate the Pre-Shared Key~$PSK$ from the Passphrase and Service Set Identification~$SSID$, defined as follows:
\begin{equation}
    PSK = \text{PBKDF2}(Passphrase, SSID)
\end{equation}
Passphrase is a user-defined secret phrase consisting of 8 to 63 characters, while $SSID$ represents the network name, which is typically public. Notably, if a user prefer to selecting the Passphrase from a limited character set, such as simple numerical combinations, the security of the 4-way handshake protocol will be easily compromised by attackers, which we will discuss in \autoref{sec:wpa2-psk}.

In WPA2-PSK, the Pairwise Master Key~$PMK$ is equivalent to $PSK$.
Based on the $PMK$, the authenticator and supplicant perform the 4-way handshake.
First, the authenticator and supplicant exchange random values $N_A$ and $N_B$ in message 1 and 2. 
Then they calculate $PTK$ as follows:
\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
        PTK = \text{PRF}(PMK,{MAC}_{A},{MAC}_\text{S}, N_A, N_B)
    \end{split} 
\end{equation}
Here, $PRF$ is a pseudo-random function. 
${MAC}_{A}$ and ${MAC}_\text{S}$ denote the MAC addresses of the authenticator and supplicant, respectively.
The $PTK$ is then used to ensure the secrecy and integrity of the communication between the supplicant and authenticator.
Subsequently, the authenticator transmits the $GTK$ to the supplicant in message 3, which is used as an encryption key in multicast communications.
Finally, the supplicant sends message 4 to the authenticator to confirm the handshake's integrity.
It is important to note that, according to the 802.1x standard, the 4-way handshake is composed of four EAPOL-key type messages, with the first EAPOL-key message incorporating an additional variable, $PMKID$, calculated as follows:
\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
        PMKID = \text{HMAC}(PMK, {MAC}_{A} \| {MAC}_{S})
    \end{split} 
\end{equation}
The $PMKID$ here assists devices in rapidly completing authentication when reconnecting.

\subsubsection{Bluetooth Low Energy}
% Bluetooth是一种短距离无线通信技术，旨在替代有线连接，实现设备间的数据交换和通信。Bluetooth由Bluetooth Special Interest Group (SIG)维护和推广，其主要特点包括低功耗、低成本和高兼容性。Bluetooth协议栈包括多个层次，从物理层（Physical Layer, PHY）到应用层（Application Layer），每一层次都有特定的功能和协议。根据数据速率和功耗的不同，Bluetooth技术分为两大类，即Bluetooth Classic（传统蓝牙）和Bluetooth Low Energy（BLE，低功耗蓝牙），在后文中我们将以BC代指Bluetooth Classic，以BLE代指Bluetooth Low Energy。
Bluetooth is a short-range wireless communication technology designed to replace wired connections, facilitating data exchange and communication between devices.
The Bluetooth Special Interest Group~(SIG) is responsible for maintaining and promoting this technology.
The primary characteristics of Bluetooth include low power consumption, cost-effectiveness, and high compatibility.
Based on variations in data rate and power consumption, Bluetooth technology is categorized into two types: 
Bluetooth Classic~(BC), which is appropriate for high-bandwidth applications, such as audio streaming, 
and Bluetooth Low Energy~(BLE), which was first introduced in Bluetooth 4.0\cite{bluetooth4} and is specifically engineered for low-power applications, 
making it particularly suitable for sensors and Internet of Things~(IoT) devices.
In the subsequent discussion, we will refer to Bluetooth Classic as BC, while BLE will denote Bluetooth Low Energy.

Bluetooth utilizes a pairing mechanism in both BLE and BC to share a long-term key between devices, thereby ensuring the secrecy and integrity of data transmission.
First we take a look at the pairing process between BLE devices.

\begin{figure}[hbtp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.912689\columnwidth]{img/ble-pairing} 
    \caption{BLE pairing}
    \label{fig:ble-pairing}
\end{figure}

The pairing process between two BLE devices, designated as the Initiator and the Responder, involves three phases, as depicted in \autoref{fig:ble-pairing}.
In the first phase, the devices exchange pairing features, which include the IO capabilities and the length of the Long Term Key~$LTK$.
During the second phase, the two devices conduct a pairing scheme determined by IO capabilities exchanged in last phase.
The pairing schemes include Just Works, Passkey Entry, and Out of Band.
At the end of this phase, both devices successfully negotiate~$LTK$.
In the final phase, the Initiator and Responder utilize the $LTK$ to derive a Session Key$SK$.
This $SK$ is then employed to secure the channel over which transport-specific keys are distributed, such as the Identity Resolving Key$IRK$ and Identity Address information.

\subsubsection{Bluetooth Classic}
BC, also referred to as Basic Rate/Enhanced Date Rate~(BR/EDR), represents the initial Bluetooth standard, 
predominantly designed for applications requiring high data transfer rates such as audio streaming , file transfer and serial communication.
The BC standard delineates link layer security mechanisms that ensure secrecy, integrity and authentication for higher-layer applications.
Pairing is employed to negotiate a long-term link key~LK between tow Bluetooth devices.
Subsequent to the pairing procedure, the session establishment protocol enables devices to derive a Session Key~SK from the LK and other parameters, to safeguard subsequent communication. \autoref{fig:bc-session} illustrates this phase between paired BC devices.
The devices involved in BC session establishment are designated as the central device and the peripheral device.
Prior to the initiation of the session, both entities share~LK.

\begin{figure}[hbtp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.902212\columnwidth]{img/bc-session} 
    \caption{BC Session}
    \label{fig:bc-session}
\end{figure}

Before establishing the encrypted session, the central device and peripheral build a link connection first. 
In this phase, the devices exchange features supported by them, like $FeatSC$ which means whether the device supports \emph{Secure Connections}.
After the link connection is established, the two devices start the authentication process. 
There are two methods to authenticate the devices:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Legacy Authentication}: Only the central device will authenticate the peripheral device.
    \item \textbf{Secure Authentication}: Both devices will authenticate each other.
\end{itemize}
In the both authentication methods, the two devices would use $LK$ to compute the Authenticated Channel Offset~$ACO$ based on the random value(s).
If both devices' $FeatSC$ is \emph{True}, they will use the secure authentication method, otherwise, they will use the legacy authentication method.
In the following phase, the central and peripheral devices engage in negotiation regarding the length of the session key.
In the final phase, the central device send an random value~$EnRand$, to the peripheral device.
The two devices then utilize the $LK$, $EnRand$ and $ACO$ to derive the session key~$SK'$.
But in \emph{Secure Connections}, $EnRand$ will be ignored by the peripheral device and the $SK'$ will be derived from $LK$ and $ACO$.
After that, the central device and peripheral device will take a few bytes of $SK'$ as the session key~$SK$ based on the negotiated key length.
The peripheral device then encrypts an \Tfunction{ACCEPT} message using $SK$ and sends it to the central device.
The successful decryption of this message by the central device signifies the establishment of the encrypted session.

\subsection{TAMARIN Prover}
The TAMARIN prover provides a powerful and expressive language for modeling protocols and properties, allowing researchers to analyze the security of their designs rigorously.
TAMARIN supports a wide range of cryptographic primitives and protocols, making it a versatile tool for formal analysis.
In TAMARIN, we use terms to represent messages and the behavior of protocols and adversaries are captured through multiset rewriting rules. 
TAMARIN also allows using logic formulas to specific security properties of protocols.

\subsubsection{Messages and term algebra}
Messages in symbolic models are typically represent as terms in a term algebra. 
A term is an expression built from symbols that encode the data exchanged in the protocol. 
The symbolic model abstracts away the concrete cryptographic operations, representing them through algebraic terms that capture their logical structure.
For example, the encryption of a message~$m$ with a key~$k$ can be represented as a term:~$\text{senc}(m, k)$. 
Here,~$\text{senc}$ is a function symbol representing symmetric encryption.
Formally, we assume a signature~$\Sigma$ defining the set of such function or operator symbols and their arities and a countably infinite set of variables~$\mathcal{V}$. 
Similarly, a countable set~$\mathcal{N}$ is defined as the set of names(like keys, nonces, public identifiers etc.). 
The set of terms is the closure of~$\mathcal{V}$ and~$\mathcal{N}$ under the operators in~$\Sigma$ and donated as~$T_\Sigma(\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{N})$.
A \emph{message} is a special \emph{term} which doesn't contain any variables.
We use equations to specify the algebraic properties of cryptographic functions.
An equation is a pair of terms, denoted as~$t_1 = t_2$, which specifies that the~$t_1$ can be substituted by~$t_2$, for~$t_1,t_2 \in T_\Sigma(\mathcal{V})$.
For instance, the equation~$\text{sdec}(\text{senc}(m, k), k) = m$ models the property that the decryption of an encryption with the same key yields the original message.


\subsubsection{Protocols and multiset rewriting rules}
Multiset rewriting system are used to model protocols and adversaries in TAMARIN. 
Every state of the transition system is a multiset of facts. 
A \emph{fact} is defined as~$F(t_1, \ldots, t_n)$, for~$t_i \in T_\Sigma(\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{N})$.
The transitions of states are defined by labeled multiset rewriting rules, which is denoted as~$F_l \xrightarrow{F_a} F_r$.
Here,~$F_l$,~$F_a$ and~$F_r$ are the \emph{left-hand side}, \emph{action} and \emph{right-hand side} facts of the rule, respectively.
When all of the facts in~$F_l$ are present in the current state, the rule can be applied, and the facts in~$F_a$ are consumed, while the facts in~$F_r$ are added to the new state.
There are two sorts of facts: linear facts and persistent facts.
Linear facts can only be consumed once after their creation, while persistent facts can be consumed an unlimited number of times.
Action facts in~$F_a$ represent the label of the rule and can be used to restrict executions of the rule or to verify properties of the protocol.

\subsubsection{Properties and logic formulas}
Trace properties are usually expressed as first-order logic formulas which are evaluated over the traces (states sequences) of the protocol.
The trace properties in TAMARIN are defined as first-order logic formulas with time points using action facts.
When it comes to cryptographic protocols, secrecy or authentication are usually included in the trace properties to be verified.
For example, the secrecy requires that a secret message~$m$ cannot be known by adversaries in any trace of the protocol.
This property can be encoded in the following lemma:
\begin{flalign*}
    & \hspace{0em} \text{\TLemma{All}} \;\, \text{\Tterm{m \#i.}} \;\, \text{\TFact{Send(m)@i}} \implies \neg (\text{\TLemma{Ex \#j.}} \;\, \text{\TFact{K(m)@j}} ) &\\
\end{flalign*}
This formula describes that for all time points~$i$, if a message~$m$ is sent at time~$i$, then there doesn't exist a time point~$j$ at which the message~$m$ is known by an adversary.
