\section*{Ethics Considerations}

\noindent
\textbf{Responsible disclosure.}
We reported the UMD vulnerability to the Bluetooth Special Interest Group~(SIG) security team on July~15, 2024.
The SIG acknowledged receipt and, in their February~7, 2025 response, stated that ``the scenario posited for identifying the session key used during BR/EDR encryption establishment appears much more practical'' than previously known attacks.
The SIG is conducting a detailed analysis and will provide further feedback.

\noindent
\textbf{Experimental setup.}
All analysis in this paper is conducted entirely within the symbolic model using \Tamarin{}.
No real Bluetooth devices were involved in the discovery or validation of the UMD attack.
The attack traces are produced by the automated verifier and do not require physical proximity to any device.

\noindent
\textbf{User-side risk assessment.}
The UMD attack targets Bluetooth Classic peripheral devices that accept session keys shorter than 7~bytes.
Until specification updates and firmware patches are deployed, users can mitigate risk by: (i)~avoiding pairing with untrusted or unknown devices, (ii)~preferring devices that enforce Secure Connections and maximum key lengths, and (iii)~monitoring for unexpected re-pairing events that may indicate an active downgrade attack.
