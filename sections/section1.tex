\section{Introduction}

% A key with high entropy demotes a larger key space, thereby augmenting the difficulty for an attacker to recover the key through brute-force methods.

% Unfortunately, some vulnerabilities in protocols associated with low-entropy keys have been exploited by attackers, exemplified by the PMKID \cite{pmkidattack} and KNOB \cite{antonioli2019knob} attacks.

% Second, current versions of such protocols might maintain compatibility with these obsolete designs for the sake of backward compatibility.
% Third, some protocols opt for shorter keys to cater to devices with limited computational or power resources \cite{bluetooth6}.

Entropy is a fundamental concept which is directly correlated with the security of the cryptographic system, where a key with high entropy denotes difficulty for an attacker to recover the key from the ciphertext.
However, in practice, modern cryptographic protocols, such as WPA2-PSK, Bluetooth Low Energy~(BLE) and Bluetooth Classic~(BC), usually tolerate low-entropy keys to accommodate user-friendly designs, backward compatibility, or resource-constrained devices.
This introduces a security issue about recovering low-entropy keys in these protocols, such as PMKID~\cite{pmkidattack} and KNOB~\cite{antonioli2019knob} attacks.

The use of symbolic models to formally analyze protocol vulnerabilities, pioneered by Dolev et al.~\cite{dolev1983security}, has become a mainstream research approach.
% Many symbolic model tools, such as~\cite{armando2005avispa,blanchet2018proverif,escobar2007maude,cremers2008scyther,meier2013tamarin,cspa}, have been developed to automatically analyze protocols and discover attacks.
Numbers tools have been developed to automatically analyze protocols and identify potential attacks, including AVISPA\cite{armando2005avispa}, ProVerif~\cite{blanchet2018proverif}, Maude-NPA~\cite{escobar2007maude}, Scyther~\cite{cremers2008scyther}, TAMARIN~\cite{meier2013tamarin}, and others.
In symbolic models, messages and keys are abstracted as terms in term algebra rather than as concrete bit sequences, as used in computational models. 
Consequentially, the entropy of keys is lost during this abstraction process, making it impossible for existing symbolic model tools to distinguish low-entropy keys or to analyze the brute-force capabilities of attackers.

This raises a critical question: how can one capture the brute-force behaviors of attackers within symbolic models?
Some studies\cite{shi2023blesc, claverie2023tamarin} have proposed solutions by manually identifying keys that are vulnerable to compromise and defining specific rules that allow these keys to be directly revealed to attackers.
However, these studies do not provide a general or automated method for analyzing the impact of low-entropy keys on the whole protocol. 
% For one thing, this approach requires manual analysis of the vulnerabilities inherent in the protocol keys instead of automated analysis.
% For another, manual analysis is inherently constrained, as it can only provide attacker with limited ways to compromise low-entropy keys and fails to provide a comprehensive strategy for attackers to exploit low-entropy keys effectively.
Firstly, this method relies on manual identification of key vulnerabilities, limiting the scalability and objectivity of the analysis.
Secondly, manual analysis is inherently constrained, as it only offers attacks a limited set of predefined ways to compromise low-entropy keys.
As a result, it fails to provide a comprehensive strategy for effectively exploiting such vulnerabilities.

To automatically analyze protocols tolerating low-entropy keys, we face three main challenges:
% First, the symbolic analysis tools only remain the logical structure of messages or keys, and the entropy of keys is hard to be expressed and analyzed. 
% Second, brute-force attacks require three prerequisites: known encryption algorithm, known ciphertext, and no additional values~(salt value, IV, etc.) required for calculating the ciphertext. 
% These prerequisites are ambiguous, and it is hard to capture different numbers of additional values  and cryptographic operators in a single rule.
% Third, adversaries' brute force ability needs to be improved, no longer targeting a specific key, but can be freely combined with other capabilities and applied to the attack.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Entropy Representation:}
    Symbolic analysis tools only preserve the logical structure of messages or keys, making it impossible to deduce and analyze the entropy of keys.
    This abstraction significantly limits the ability of symbolic models to analyze the strength of keys. 
    Additional mechanisms are needed to describe the key's entropy in order to cover general brute-force attacks within the symbolic model.
    \item \textbf{Prerequisites for Brute-Force Attacks:}
    Brute-force attacks typically require three essential prerequisites: knowledge of the algorithm, access to the ciphertext, and knowledge of additional values~(e.g., salt values, initialization vectors). 
    These prerequisites are general and abstract, and the specific requirements that an attacker must meet vary significantly across different scenarios.
    In symbolic models, it is a challenge to define a set of general rules that can accurately describe these prerequisites.
    \item \textbf{Enhanced Adversarial Capabilities:}
    Existing models for certain protocols allow attackers to compromise specific keys, which strictly limits the attacker's ability to break the key. 
    In the real world, however, as long as the attacker satisfies the three prerequisites, they can launch a brute-force attack, even though success is not guaranteed. 
    To better reflect realistic scenarios, it is necessary to grant attackers more flexibility in performing brute-force attacks, enabling them to freely select the keys to target and combine this with other attack capabilities.
\end{enumerate}

% 我们如何解决这些挑战，我们是怎么做的
This paper aims to bridge this gap by introducing a generalized and automated method for modeling low-entropy keys and the adversary's brute-force capabilities within symbolic analysis frameworks. 
We focus on enhancing the TAMARIN prover, to support the modeling of low-entropy keys and to describe generalized brute-force attacks by adversaries. 
TAMARIN is a state-of-the-art protocol verification tool widely used for formal analysis of many protocols, including Bluetooth\cite{shi2023blesc,claverie2023tamarin, jangid2023bluepe}, TLS\cite{cremers2017tls13}, 5G-AKA\cite{basin20185G, wang20215G}, EMV\cite{basin2021emv,radu2022emv}, etc.
Our methodology involves extending the TAMARIN prover's syntax with new keywords to explicitly mark low-entropy keys within protocols. 
We develop a compilation tool that translates the extended syntax into formal models recognizable by the TAMARIN prover. 
This tool automatically generates necessary facts and rules, including key-breaking oracles that simulate an adversary's ability to compromise low-entropy keys when certain conditions are met.
Our methodology can be employed to identify and address security issues arising from low entropy within protocols.

% 有什么效果，结论，发现。
\noindent\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item  We propose a general method to model low-entropy keys and brute-force attacks in symbolic models, enabling automated identification of vulnerable keys within protocols.
    \item We enhance the Tamarin prover's modeling language by introducing new syntax and develop a compilation tool to facilitate the translation of extended models, improving usability and automation.
    \item Applying our method to real-world protocols such as 4-way-handshake protocol of WPA2-PSK, BLE pairing protocol and BC session establishment protocol, we re-discover known vulnerabilities in these protocols, including the PMKID and KNOB attacks.
    we demonstrate its effectiveness by re-discovering known vulnerabilities, including the PMKID and KNOB attacks, and previously undetected attacks, the unilateral multi-downgrade(UMD) attack against BR/EDR. 
    \item We discovered a novel attack against the BC session establishment protocol, the unilateral multi-downgrade(UMD) attack, which significantly reduces the difficulty for attackers to brute force the complete session keys. This attack induces one side of the devices to use keys of different downgraded-lengths multiple times, potentially reducing the computational complexity to as low as $\frac{1}{2^{116}}$ of the normal scenario.
\end{itemize}
