\section{Discussion and Conclusion} \label{sec:8discussion-conclusion}
\textbf{Discussion.} 
Although we imposed restrictions on the key-cracking oracle, in some cases, verification may either fail to terminate or take an excessively long time. 
Automated verification requires an appropriate verification tactic to proceed effectively. 
Even so, compared to models without low-entropy labels, the complexity of analyzing models with low-entropy keys increases significantly, leading to varying degrees of increased verification time.

In our methodology, we did not consider private functions, as our methodology is based on public protocols. 
In practical applications, we can model private functions by replacing their implementations with a combination of public functions and private variables. 
We plan to  consider adding support for private functions to our tool in the future.

\textbf{Conclusion.} 
We proposed the first methodology to analyze the impact of adversarial computation capabilities on the protocols that tolerant low-entropy keys. 
Our method can determine whether a protocol using a certain key length is secure and under what level of adversarial computation capabilities  it remains secure.
Our case studies illustrate our methodology is effective in discovering potential weaknesses in protocols, and it can automatically detect brute-force attacks that previously could only be identified through manual analysis.
The novel attacks discovered through case studies fully demonstrate the advantages of our method over manual analysis.
It can fully leverage various attacker capabilities to identify weaknesses in protocols.
