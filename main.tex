\title{Small Leaks Sink Great Ships:\\ Automated Analysis of Protocols that Tolerate Low-Entropy Keys}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous Authors}
    \IEEEauthorblockA{Affiliation withheld for double-blind review}}


\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Modern cryptographic protocols such as WPA2-PSK, Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE), and Bluetooth Classic (BC) tolerate low-entropy keys for user-friendliness, backward compatibility, or resource-constrained deployment, yet existing symbolic protocol verifiers rely on the perfect-encryption assumption and therefore cannot reason about brute-force attackers.
Prior work addresses this gap only through protocol-specific, manually encoded reveal rules, which neither generalize across protocols nor systematically explore compound-key decomposition.
We introduce the first protocol-agnostic, automated symbolic methodology for low-entropy keys, built on three abstractions: the message-derivation fact \texttt{MDerive}, which captures brute-force prerequisites independently of concrete function symbols; a three-tier breaking-oracle design (value-breaking, relationship-breaking, message-derivation) that composes with the standard Dolev-Yao attacker; and a graded bit-level attacker model that anchors verification results to a concrete computational budget.
We realize the methodology as EntropyVerif, a syntax-sugar extension of \Tamarin{} that requires no kernel modification.
Using EntropyVerif we automatically rediscover the PMKID, KNOB, and Method Confusion attacks, and uncover a new attack on BC which we call the Unilateral Multi-Downgrade (UMD) attack: by repeatedly downgrading one side of a BC session and recombining the recovered key fragments, an attacker reduces session-key recovery against devices using the maximum 128-bit key from $2^{128}$ to $n \cdot 2^{128/n}$, bottoming out at $2^{12}$ for $n=16$---a factor of $2^{116}$ reduction that has been responsibly disclosed to the vendor.
Across three real-world protocols and six BC device configurations, our results show that entropy-aware symbolic analysis is both feasible and necessary.
\end{abstract}
